What does a home sauna cost to run in Dubai?

People budget carefully for the cabin and then worry quietly about the bill. Here is the arithmetic, with DEWA’s own residential slabs.

Dubai, UAE · Updated November 2026

A home sauna in Dubai costs a few dirhams per session to run — for most households, under AED 50 a month. It is one of the cheapest things in a villa with a heating element in it, and it is nowhere near the pool pump or the air conditioning.

Here is how that number is arrived at, so you can redo it for your own usage rather than taking it on trust.

What the heater actually draws

Our two-person cabin runs a Harvia 4.5 kW heater; the four-person runs a 6.0 kW. Both are configurable for a single-phase supply, up to 8.5 kW.

The important thing is that a heater does not draw its rated power continuously. It draws it hard while bringing the cabin up to temperature, then cycles — on, off, on — to hold it. Over a session, a reasonable working assumption is full draw during heat-up and roughly a third to a half of it while holding.

What DEWA charges for a kilowatt-hour

DEWA bills residential electricity on rising slabs — you pay each band's rate only on the units that fall inside it. As published for 2026:

Monthly consumptionRate
0 – 2,000 kWh23 fils / kWh
2,001 – 4,000 kWh28 fils / kWh
4,001 – 6,000 kWh32 fils / kWh
Above 6,000 kWh38 fils / kWh

On top of the slab there is a fuel surcharge, which DEWA sets monthly and which has recently been in the region of 6 fils per kWh, plus a small meter charge and 5% VAT.

DEWA moves the fuel surcharge every month, so treat the figures below as the right order of magnitude rather than a quotation. The current rates are on DEWA's own tariff page, and your bill shows which slab your household actually sits in.

Putting the two together

Take a villa that already uses enough electricity to sit in the third slab — 32 fils, plus roughly 6 fils of fuel surcharge, plus VAT. Call it 40 fils per kWh all in. That is a deliberately unfavourable assumption; a flat or a smaller villa pays less.

A household in the lowest slab, at 23 fils plus surcharge, would pay roughly a third less again.

Where that sits on the bill

For scale: a single split air-conditioning unit running through a Dubai summer afternoon will go through more electricity in a day than a sauna used three times a week gets through in a month. A pool pump running eight hours a day is in a different league entirely.

If a sauna pushes your household into the next DEWA slab, something else on the bill was already doing the heavy lifting.

Four things that genuinely reduce it

  • Do not pre-heat earlier than you need to. The heat-up is most of the cost of a session. Forty minutes before, not two hours before. This is the single biggest lever, and it is free.
  • Use it in summer. A cabin starting from 40°C needs materially less energy than one starting from 18°C. The season people worry about is the cheap one.
  • Run sessions back to back. Two people one after the other costs barely more than one, because the cabin is already hot. The second session is close to free.
  • Insulation, not wattage. How well the cabin holds heat decides how often the heater cycles. It is the part of the build you cannot upgrade later, which is why it is not where to economise.

Does the WiFi panel cost more to run?

No — it tends to cost less. The AED 4,000 WiFi control panel lets you start the cabin from your phone, which means you start it forty minutes before you actually arrive rather than leaving it running because you are not sure when you will get home. The panel itself draws a negligible amount on standby.

What about an infrared cabin?

Infrared panels run at lower power and heat you rather than the room, so a session draws less. They also deliver a different experience — lower air temperature, no steam, no ladle. Which one suits you is a question about what you want from it, not about the bill; we set the two side by side in traditional, infrared or hybrid.

And the supply itself

None of this matters if the cabin cannot be connected. Our saunas are configurable for a single-phase supply up to 8.5 kW, which most villas and many apartments already have headroom for — but it needs checking before anything is ordered.

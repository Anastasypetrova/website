Building a sauna into a room you already have

The best place for a sauna is often somewhere that is currently storing suitcases.

Dubai, UAE · Updated November 2026

A sauna built into an existing room starts at AED 25,000 and is quoted after a site visit. It is the option for a space a standard cabin will not sit in politely: an alcove that is 1.4 m wide, a shower nobody has used in two years, the dead corner of a home gym.

The two cabins we build to a fixed size — two-person at AED 15,000, four-person at AED 20,000 — cover most terraces and gardens. This page is about the other case.

What "custom" actually means

Not a different product. The same construction, the same Harvia heater, the same bench detail — drawn to your dimensions instead of ours. What changes is that every panel is cut for the room, and the room's own awkwardness becomes the brief: the pipe boxing in the corner, the step up at the threshold, the window you want to keep.

A cabin that fits a room exactly looks built-in rather than parked. That is most of why people choose it.

Rooms that work

The second shower

The most common one by a distance. A villa or an apartment with more bathrooms than it needs has a shower that has become a store cupboard. It is already tiled, already drained, already has a wet-rated electrical supply nearby, and it is a sealed box of roughly the right size. Half the preparation is already done.

A corner of the gym

The second most common. The sauna is a step away from where you train, which is the difference between using it twice a week and using it on Sundays. Gyms also tend to have the electrical headroom already.

An alcove or a recess

The kind of dead space a floor plan leaves behind: the end of a corridor, the gap beside a staircase, a recess under a mezzanine. These are the jobs that look impossible on the phone and turn out fine with a tape measure.

A balcony or terrace nook

A corner that a free-standing cabin would not fit, but a built one will. Outdoors brings its own questions — sun, dust, what the building allows — and those are in can you put a sauna on a balcony in Dubai.

How it is done

  1. Site visit. We measure the room, look at the floor, the power and the route in, and talk about how you expect to use it. Two people or four changes the bench layout entirely.
  2. Design. Drawn to the room: internal dimensions, bench heights and depths, where the door swings, where the heater goes and how far it must sit from the timber.
  3. Fixed-price quotation. One number for the whole job, after we have seen the space. Not before.
  4. Build. Cut by the team that will install it.
  5. Installation. Assembled in the room, connected to the isolator your electrician has left, heated with you there, and handed over with a walkthrough.

Everything carries the same cover as our standard cabins: five years on workmanship, two years manufacturer's warranty on the electrical components.

What the room needs

Less than people fear. The constraints are the same as for any sauna and are set out in full in what a sauna needs before it can be installed. In short:

  • Height. Around 2.1–2.2 m of clear internal height is comfortable. Lower can be done; it changes the bench.
  • Power. A single-phase supply, up to 8.5 kW, with an isolator near the cabin.
  • A level, solid floor. Tiled or screeded is ideal and usually already there.
  • Somewhere for the heat to go afterwards. The room should not be hermetically sealed; a bathroom extract or a window is plenty.

What it does not need: plumbing, drainage, or a specific orientation. A traditional sauna produces no run-off — the water you ladle onto the stones leaves as steam.

Why it costs more

AED 25,000 against AED 15,000 is a fair question. Three reasons, and none of them is that the materials are better:

  • It is drawn from nothing. A standard cabin is built from a drawing that exists. Yours does not yet.
  • Nothing is repeated. Every panel, bench and trim is cut once, for one room.
  • It is built in place. A finished cabin cannot be carried through a doorway into an alcove, so the work happens on site over more days.

The number is fixed before anything starts, and it does not move afterwards.

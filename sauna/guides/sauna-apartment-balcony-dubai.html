Can you put a sauna in an apartment or on a balcony in Dubai?

The answer is usually yes, and the three things that decide it have nothing to do with the sauna itself.

Dubai, UAE · Updated November 2026

A sauna can go in a Dubai apartment or on a balcony more often than people expect. It is not a swimming pool. A two-person cabin is 1.2 × 1.2 m on the floor and runs from a single-phase supply. The obstacles, when there are any, are structural, electrical or administrative — and all three are knowable before you commit to anything.

The three questions that decide it

1. Will the floor take it

A sauna is heavy, but it is heavy over a known footprint, which is what matters. A loaded bookcase or a full bathtub puts more load through less floor. On a balcony, what matters is the slab and how the load is spread across it — and balconies in towers are designed with a live load in mind that a small cabin plus two people rarely troubles.

We take the dimensions and the weight at the site visit, and give you the figures in writing. If your building asks, those are the numbers it will want.

2. Is there power to reach it

Our saunas are configurable for a single-phase supply, up to 8.5 kW. A two-person cabin runs a Harvia 4.5 kW heater and a four-person a 6.0 kW. Most apartments have enough headroom on the board; what needs checking is the headroom and whether a circuit can be run to where the sauna will stand.

You provide an isolator near the sauna, ready for connection — a licensed electrician's job. What a sauna needs before installation sets out what to ask for.

3. What does the building allow

This is the one nobody can answer in the abstract, and the one worth asking about first.

Developers and building managements in Dubai take different positions on what can be installed on a balcony or fitted inside a leased apartment, and a freehold villa community is a different conversation again from a tower. Some care about anything visible from outside. Some care about electrical work. Some do not care at all.

Ask your developer or building management early, before any money moves. We give you the dimensions, the weight and the electrical load they will ask for — that is usually the whole of what they need to answer.

Balcony or inside?

Both work, and they fail for different reasons.

A balcony gives you somewhere to step out to afterwards and keeps the heat out of your living space entirely. Against it: the sun, the dust, and the possibility that the building has an opinion about the balcony's appearance. A cabin in shade — under the overhang, against the party wall — ages better and is more comfortable to open. See does an outdoor sauna work in the Dubai summer.

Inside is quieter administratively and often easier. The places that work in a Dubai apartment are the same ones that work in a villa: a second bathroom nobody uses, a walk-in shower that has become storage, a corner of a home gym, the end of a utility room. Air conditioning means the cabin starts from 22°C rather than 40°C, costing a few more minutes to heat — see what a home sauna costs to run.

If the space is an odd shape, a standard cabin may not fit and a sauna built to the room will.

Ventilation indoors

A sauna does not vent into the room like a steam shower. It is a dry-heat cabin, and the moisture in play is whatever you ladle onto the stones. What it does need is to be allowed to air out after use: leave the door open for a while, and make sure the room it sits in is not a sealed box. In practice, an ordinary bathroom extract or a window is plenty.

Getting it in

Worth thinking about before anything else. A cabin that cannot be carried in assembled is built in place, which is longer but entirely normal — we have done it in apartments reached only by a service lift. What we look at on the visit is the narrowest point of the route: the lift, the turn at the top of a stairwell, the front door, the balcony threshold.

If the route is tight, say so when you call. It changes the plan, not the answer.

What about renting?

If you rent, two extra things matter: your landlord's permission, and whether the sauna can come with you. An outdoor cabin on a balcony is a free-standing object and can be moved; a sauna built into an alcove is part of the apartment from the day it is finished. Which of the two you want is worth deciding before the design, not after.

Want one of your own?

We check the floor, the power and the access on the site visit, before anything is quoted or ordered. If your building turns out to be a no, you will know early and it will have cost you nothing.

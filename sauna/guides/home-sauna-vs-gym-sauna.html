Home sauna or the one at the gym?

There is a sauna in your building. There is one at the gym. The question is not which is hotter — it is which one you will actually use.

Dubai, UAE · Updated November 2026

Almost every tower in Dubai has a sauna in the spa, and almost every gym has one. So the honest version of this question is: why would anyone spend AED 15,000 on something they can already use for nothing?

Four answers, and one argument for the gym.

1. The one you use is the one twenty steps away

This is the whole thing, and everything else is detail.

A sauna at the gym requires a trip. The trip requires a decision, a bag, a car or a lift, and a block of time. A sauna on your terrace requires standing up. The difference does not sound like much until you count sessions over a year.

Clients who had been using a gym sauna once a week tell us they end up using their own three or four times — not because they became more disciplined, but because the friction went. A sauna is a habit, and habits lose to inconvenience every time. The text in Heat and the body on our own site makes the same point: it is the compounding that matters, not one dramatic session.

2. The temperature is actually yours

Shared saunas are run at whatever temperature causes the fewest complaints, which is usually around 70°C, and in buildings where people shower in them and prop the door open, often less. A Finnish sauna is a different experience at 90°C than at 70°C, and you cannot get there in a room you do not control.

Nor can you throw water on the stones. In most building saunas that is either forbidden or impossible — and löyly is the part that makes a traditional sauna what it is. See traditional, infrared or hybrid.

3. Nobody else is in it

An obvious one that people are too polite to say first. A building sauna means other people's conversations, other people's phones, other people's idea of what to wear, and waiting while a class empties into it. Hygiene in a shared sauna is a matter of who used it before you.

At home the room is yours, at the hour you want it, with whoever you want in it.

4. The hours

Building spas close. Gyms close, or at least their quiet hours do not coincide with yours. A sauna in the garden has no hours. For anyone who finishes work at nine, this is not a small point — and the evening is exactly when a sauna in Dubai is most pleasant to use.

The electricity figure comes from working a session through DEWA's residential slabs in what a home sauna costs to run. Nobody joins a gym purely for the sauna, so this is not a fair fight in either direction — but it does put AED 15,000 in proportion. It is roughly two years of a mid-range membership, and it does not stop when you stop paying.

When the gym is genuinely the better answer

We would rather say this than sell someone a cabin that ends up as an expensive shed.

  • You have not used the gym sauna this month. Convenience multiplies an existing habit; it does not create one. If the sauna at the gym does not pull you in, a sauna at home will not either.
  • You move every year. A free-standing cabin can come with you, but it is still a thing to move. A sauna built into a room cannot come at all.
  • You are renting short-term. Your landlord's permission plus a one-year lease is a difficult combination.
  • There is genuinely nowhere to put it. It happens, and a site visit will say so.

The quiet one: what it does to the evening

The argument clients make a year in is rarely about money or temperature. It is that having one changes the shape of the evening — there is somewhere to go that is not a screen, twenty steps away, with nothing to book and nowhere to drive.

That is not something a spreadsheet captures, and it is the reason the cabins get used far more than anyone expects when they order one.
